# QuestBank

The free questing addon behind https://foreverrank.com/questbank/. Both factions, levels 1 to 60:
what every quest pays at your level, which chains to carry on, the best quests near you, the hand-in route with map
pins, party sync, and banking with a hand-in run when the game holds your level at a cap (QB:Mode() "lock", "rush",
"quest"). Wowhead Forever's quests seed the catalog up to the beta's cap; above it the CMaNGOS Classic database does,
flagged "Classic only" until players see the quests in Forever.
Free software under the GPL-3.0 (`QuestBank/LICENSE.txt`): its quest chains and spawn points come from the
CMaNGOS Classic database, which is GPL-3.0. The minimap's far zoom, its tile table, the look of its markers, strokes and
step circles, the compass dial of the arrow and all of that art (`QuestBank/art/*.png`, drawn by Krom) come from QuestTrail
3.0.6 by Krom under the MIT licence (`QuestBank/LICENSE-QuestTrail.txt`; each such file says so at its top).

The addon, `QuestBank/`:

- `Data.lua` (generated): the quest catalog, NPCs, flight network, categories, textures and icons.
- `Core.lua`: game state, quest status, the plan, the hand-in run, the XP the game reports, settings, events, export.
- `Discover.lua`: what the game shows while you quest (quests, the NPCs who give and take them and where they stand, offers, chains, XP, reward items), in `QuestBankDB.disc`, stamped with the client's build and interface number.
- `Survey.lua`: the quest survey, on the Forever client while Settings' "Learn quests from the game" is ticked (it is by default): out of combat, outside instances and from half a minute after login (ten seconds after a loading screen), it asks the game about the quests of `D.SURVEY` by id, two a second with at most three waiting (`C_QuestLog.RequestLoadQuestByID`, answered by `QUEST_DATA_LOAD_RESULT`; a quest the client has already is read without a question), and reads the title, the reward items to choose from and always given (`GetQuestLogChoiceInfo` / `GetQuestLogRewardInfo` by quest id, read again until `HaveQuestRewardData`), the XP and money at your level and the quest's level. Kept once per account and build in `QuestBankDB.disc.survey` (a failed try three times and the quest rests until the next build), counted in `QuestBankDB.diag.survey`, `/qb survey` prints the progress, `/qb survey find <text>` the quests read whose title has the text, with their rewards. Used at once: a catalog quest with no rewards known, or only the Classic database's, takes the game's lists (the finders, tooltips and Available), and a quest no list has, with reward items, a level and a real title, shows in the reward finders as "Seen in game" with no giver, place or side.
- `Game.lua`: on the Forever client only (interface 16xxx), and while Settings' "Note items, spells and loot" is ticked: per class and race the spells learned and the items worn and carried, and the tooltips of the weapons, armor and recipes you loot, carry or see: every item a loot window shows (through `Loot.lua`) or the game announces to the group as a boss drop, everything worn and carried (looked through at login and a few seconds after the bags change), vendors' wares and anything hovered, read two every half second out of combat; a server-sent item the client hasn't loaded is asked for (`C_Item.RequestLoadItemDataByID`, read on `ITEM_DATA_LOAD_RESULT`) and nothing half-read is kept: a tooltip the server hasn't filled in, one the client returns nothing or no lines for, or one with a line or a line's text hidden is read again a few times and otherwise left for the next sweep (at most 1,000 kept, read again after six hours), in `QuestBankDB.game`. No names. Unticking the box clears what was noted, since `QuestBank.lua` is uploaded whole. It also takes over, once, the notes of the addon it replaces, and once they are in, tells players still running that one that its folder can go.
- `Loot.lua`: on the Forever client only, under the same box: which creature or chest dropped which items, by id, with the dungeon, difficulty and boss fight (or the map outdoors; neither when the client hides the place); at most 400 sources and 40 items each, in `QuestBankDB.disc.loot`. It only reads the loot window (never takes, closes or rolls), and keeps no name: the site names the ids through the client's encounter table and the Classic database. Unticking the box clears these too.
- `Model.lua`: Forever quest XP, travel over the flight network, and the route planner (planned a few milliseconds a frame). Once the client has said which flight paths this character knows and one is not among them, the planner flies only those it knows (kept per character by its GUID, `Travel.lua`'s `TV.DB`: an entry kept by name before 5.4.4 keeps only its flight times and its hearthstone, and the client says the flight paths again; once the client has said anything of them this session, a flight master it has not named is not counted, where 5.4.3 counted every one on a continent it had not read; Forever's world map lists only the flight paths found, so both continents' maps answering with a list, each saying it shows flight points (`C_TaxiMap.ShouldMapShowTaxiNodes`), is that word too, empty or not: a character who has found none flies none, where a client without the calls still counts them all), by `Travel.lua`'s router (its minutes from each flight master to the others, kept until a flight path is learned or timed, or Frequent Flier's rank changes), and a route from where you stand starts at the nearest flight master you can fly from on your land (on a land with none you can fly from, Darnassus with only Rut'theran Village known or a continent before its first flight path, the nearest on the continent, as 5.4.3 had it, the router going on by the flights you know): the planner, the arrow and the minutes agree. An open map off the two continents (Zephras Isle, below) is a land of its own, walked on.
- `Target.lua`: the one target, and how it is chosen and kept (below), the jumps in place that plan the route again, and `/qb trace`.
- `Legacy.lua`: the Legacy perks' ranks from the game's trait trees (`C_Traits`, read again on the trait events; the flights' minutes, and the route with them, only when Frequent Flier's rank changed), and what they change in QuestBank's numbers: Frequent Flier's faster flights, Field Guide's shorter camp cooldown, Gourmand's longer Well Fed, Permanence, Well Rested and Talented; `/qb legacy` prints them. Also the measured pace: your run speed on foot and mounted from `GetUnitSpeed`, per character, which the route model, the arrow and the quest list use instead of the tables' 7 yards a second and 60% mount; and each flight you take is timed from takeoff to landing (`Travel.lua`), so the arrow's "Lands in" and the router count your own flights.
- `Pins.lua`, `Pins.xml`: the route's pins on the world map (QuestBank's quest icons at `mapIconPct` of the game's own art, half by default, on the game's own frames, so a click lands where it would on the game's pin), drawn as QuestBank's own frames on the map canvas (not the canvas's pins, whose AcquirePin runs a call the game blocks in combat), each the game's own pin drawn again (`Pins.xml` copies `POIButtonTemplate`, `QuestOfferPinTemplate` and `BaseMapPoiPinTemplate` region for region; `P.Atlas` sets the game's atlases at the sizes the game draws them, `P.StylePoi`, `P.StyleOffer` and `P.StyleMark` give a frame the game's quest POI, quest offer and POI pin looks, with the Recurring family for repeatables and the game's frame levels and zoom scaling). A stop is the quest POI (the turn-in glyph once its quests are done, stop 1 as the super-tracked quest), a planned pick-up the game's !. Also the switch that lets Ctrl+clicks through to the game's own map pin.
- `QuestMap.lua`: the quest icons on each zone's map, the game's pins again: its ! where quests start (red where the item that starts one drops), its quest POI where finished ones are handed in and in progress on each objective, its Dungeon pin on each entrance with how many of its quests are done; the tooltips in the game's words first ("[level] name", "- objective", "Available quest", "Ready for turn-in", the green instruction lines), QuestBank's after. The objectives' areas are the game's blue blob. The minimap and the Route page keep the cursor icons for each kind of objective (`QM.PaintKind`).
- `MapPage.lua`: the Map page of the window (Forever only): the game's own map canvas, built in Lua from Blizzard_MapCanvas at login out of combat, with five of Blizzard's data providers (explored areas, zone highlight and names, your party, the quests' blue areas) and QuestBank's layers on it, the game's pins drawn again (QuestMap.lua's and Pins.lua's, and here the flight points as the game's taxi node pins with the area label instead of a tooltip, the route's strokes and the arrow's step); crumbs (a map without art greyed), Here, the dungeons on the map, a "Game's map" button whose tooltip tells the two map keys apart (QuestBank's Map key is its own binding; the game's M always opens the game's map), the zone's levels and its quests beside it; a right-click on the map is its menu (zoom out, the game's map, your zone, a waypoint). Three of the canvas's methods are overridden so it never empties the game's shared quest caches as QuestBank. QuestBank's Map key, the sidebar and `/qb map [zone]` open it; `/qb map game` (or the Settings switch) gives them back to the game's world map; `/qb journal [name]` opens the Dungeon Journal.
- `Gear.lua`: the Gear page of the window (page 9, its own key): the character sheet's slots in the paper doll's order, two columns with the weapons under the left one, each on the slot's own paper-doll art with what you wear (icon, name in its quality colour, item level) and beside it the best upgrade you can get: a quest reward within reach (the Quest Log finder's judgement, item by item, "2 levels ahead" as set) or a dungeon drop at your level (the journal's recorded loot, judged the same way), the highest item level above what you wear; a quest's choice (one reward of several) is offered for one slot only, the one with the most to lose without it. A click puts the quest on the pick-up list (right-click: its menu) or opens the journal on the boss; Shift links the item, Ctrl tries it on (`QB.API.DressUp` where set up, else the game's `DressUpItemLink`). Refreshed when what you wear changes and as the client sends item data, a moment later.
- `Minimap.lua`, `MinimapView.lua`, `MinimapRoute.lua`, `MinimapTiles.lua`: the route, hand-ins, pick-ups, objectives and entrances on the minimap, drawn as QuestTrail draws them (Settings' "Marker style", QuestTrail by default: Krom's coloured diamonds with a glyph for the work, the elite ring, the game's ! and ?, a step circle in its quest's colour for every stop, the strokes in the steps' colours, each quest's colour handed out in the route's order and kept per character; "Game icons" keeps the game's numbered pins and cursor icons), placed and kept on its rim as QuestTrail places them; the golden path along your walked trails; your arrow over it all; its size and place; the wheel past the game's widest zoom over the game's own minimap tiles, with your party and the quest's blue areas on them; a drag that pans and eases back, and the minimap's right-click menu with every switch (after QuestTrail's). `/qb minimap debug` reports the far view's state.
- `Arrow.lua`: the direction arrow, QuestBank's own by default (the game's tutorial arrow, green ahead, gold to the side, red behind, glowing as it points ahead, pinging as you arrive and popping at a new step), or QuestTrail's compass (Krom's dial, its glass tinted the target's quest's colour, the red needle) when chosen (`arrowStyle`: Settings or the arrow's right-click menu; a save that never chose has QuestBank's), with QuestBank's steps and lines under it. A click anywhere (a quest row, a pin, a ! on the map, the minimap, a waypoint) shows that place on the arrow for now (`A:Show`): "Showing X", a right-click back to the route; it ends by itself when you get there or its quest moves on from where it stood at the click: handed in, taken, given up or complete (an item it was for in your bags that was not), and the arrow says "Back to your route" once. A click on what already stands (the hand-in of a quest you have not taken, the giver of one you hold, a row banked in your bags) ends only when you get there. A click on a place changes no plan; the ! on Available, the details' Pick up and a labelled Shift-click on a map pin put a quest on the route ("Shift-click: add to my route") or take it out (a skipped quest's is routed again: un-skipped and on the pick-up list), and a pick-up mark goes as its quest is taken. Its menu skips the quest the route leads to (`UI.SkipQuest`: out of the route for good, per character; "Route it again" from the quest's menu or the map pin's Shift-click, a quest you don't hold back on the pick-up list; giving it up in the game too is a second choice of its own, asked in the game's words). Waypoints are QuestBank's arrow, or the real TomTom's; QuestBank never sets the game's own waypoint, it prints a link that does when the player clicks it (`Core.lua`, `API.SetWaypoint`): with the session's first waypoint, with one nothing on screen shows, with a Shift-click, and on a Shift-click of the arrow while it shows the waypoint (5.4.3 printed it at every click; a place shown for now carries its own, `A.Waypoint`, and a camp seller none).
- `Auto.lua`: accepting and handing in quests at NPCs as the welcome asked (6.0) or Settings say. Accept for me is a choice: Off, Quests on my route (the pick-up list and the chains you are on), or Quests worth taking (those, and any other the planner thinks worth it: never grey, never under its low-value line; a save that had it on), with a switch for a quest outside your route: take it, or leave the game's quest window to you. A skipped quest is never taken. The game's escort prompt is left to the player.
- `Guide.lua` (6.0): the step engine, no UI: the next steps from the one target and the route (accept, objectives clustered within 150 yards, hand in, travel, Train at your class trainer, the grind step, your corpse), filled with the quests Now ranks best (`QB:Plan().auto`, up to the log's free slots minus two, never more than two levels above you, never grey unless its chain pays), each step done when the game says so; "Not now" an hour, "Drop" for good; time to level from the plan, then from the measured XP an hour; the party's steps (Sync's `1G`).
- `StepFrame.lua` (6.0): the step frame: the step now, the next two greyed, the quest-item button (a secure button, set out of a fight), the time line, a click into the window, a right-click menu; the welcome card (the auto choice, once) and the 5.x question; the reward window's pick marked with QuestBank's own glow, never chosen.
- `Tips.lua` (6.0): "Kobold Vermin: 3/8 (Kobold Camp Cleanup)" in mob and object tooltips, with the party's counts; nothing when the game hides the unit.
- `Camp.lua`: the camp bar and the missing-buffs banner (which stays while you use it: a click on one of its icons, a cast or a meal under way, the mouse on it; an icon goes once its buff is on you). It notes the merchants you open who sell camp goods (a campfire kit, Simple Wood, Flint and Tinder, the Cozy Sleeping Bag, a Well Fed food) with where they stand, in `QuestBankDB.disc.sell` (at most 60, read again on `MERCHANT_UPDATE`); a grey fire names the nearest known one with its minutes, and a click shows it on the arrow until the item is in your bags. It reads the game's own camp auras out of a fight (and again on `UNIT_AURA`): by any fire ("Campfire Nearby", 1283391) without Camp Benefits (1229741), a lit button whose click is a `/sit` macro (the game keeps sitting itself from addons), then the game's countdown ("Stay put", 1229739 or 1289723) and Camp Benefits' hour. A fire gone out where you stood still (its aura gone, or your kit's 15 minutes up) with no kit in your bags: a cook short of Simple Wood or Flint and Tinder is shown the nearest general goods vendor with its minutes (one your games have seen first, else the Classic database's, `D.CAMP_SELL`, said to be Classic data that may have moved), and a click shows it on the arrow until both are in your bags; nobody sells a kit, and one who doesn't cook is offered nothing. From level 14 with no sleeping bag, the grey bag says where its quest chain goes on (it starts at the Nailed Plank in Westfall or the Burned-Out Remains in the Barrens, `D.SLEEP_CHAIN`, one quest a side), a click shows it on the arrow; once the chain is done, a seller you have seen (Rix Xizzix in Booty Bay may sell it). With the bag, it lights standing still under three Well-Rested stacks, outside a rested place, its cooldown up. Each suggestion has its own switch in Settings (sit, wood, bagChain); nothing is said in chat.
- `Glow.lua`: the interact light. Its health bars (the game's soft-interact nameplate) are an option of their own, off; a 5.4.3 save that had the setting changed gets the player's own value back. It lights quest NPCs and objects, and a trainer only where it is of use (a profession you could learn or train a rank of, your class's trainer with new spells, read at its window); every other NPC only with "Every other NPC too".
- `Sync.lua`: sharing banks, plans, runs and reported XP with QuestBank users in the party and guild.
- `UI.lua`: the window with its nine pages in a sidebar (Now, Quest Log, Available, Route, Gear, Party, the Dungeon Journal, Map, Settings; the Journal, the Map and Gear are `DungeonMap.lua`'s, `MapPage.lua`'s and `Gear.lua`'s), the post box, and the minimap button. It opens on Now, then on the page you had open.

The route and the one target (5.4.4):

- The route on screen is the one the Route page shows (`Target.lua`, `T.Mode`): a run's rest; questing, the planned route from where you stand, your log done or not and your pick-ups (Log and pick-ups), or what is ready to hand in while the page shows Ready now (`routeQuest`); banking, what is ready to hand in, or the planned route while the page shows it (`routeMode`); with Ready now (Banked now) chosen and nothing ready, the plan, and the page shows that and says so. The map's pins, the arrow (its route mode), the minimap and the Map page all follow it, and change with the page's switch at once. (5.4.4 as first built followed what was ready whenever anything was, and a quest turning complete in Alterac sent them all back to Southshore.)
- They all lead to one target (`Target.lua`, `T.Get`): a hand-in of the route within 60 yards of you (kept to 90, and against another in reach unless that one is a quarter and 30 yards nearer; none in the air or as a ghost, nor while you bank at a cap with no run under way) first, "Hand in X here", whatever it pays; else the route's first stop; while a quest that stop takes is unfinished, a spot of one of its objectives still to do (the quest kept until its objectives there are done, it is complete or gone, or you jump in place; progress on another quest of the stop never moves it (5.4.4 as first built named the quest whose count grew near its spots while the quest kept's did not, and a mob that counts for the quest kept and drops an item for the other, the kill in one read of the log and the loot in the next, flipped the objective on every mob); the stop told as the planner tells it, by its town, else by an NPC it shares with the last one, never by its name or its first NPC, so a quest taken or handed in elsewhere in town, or at a stop out of town that names it after another NPC, keeps it; after a jump in place the quest, the spot and the hand-in are picked afresh where you land) (a finished objective's spots lead nowhere, on the map's rule: `QuestMap.lua`, `QM.OpenSpots`); and while the arrow shows the way there step by step, its step toward it (a flight master, a pier, the tram). The minimap's first stroke, its label and the golden path go there, and so does the Map page's first stroke; the arrow's step is on the minimap and the Map page whatever the arrow leads to (a hand-in, a pick-up, a place clicked), the strokes going by it only on the way to the one target; a stop in the next zone is drawn to the Map page's edge, the way it lies.
- It changes on what you do, not on where you stand. A route is planned again only when what it depends on changed (`Core.lua`'s signature): your quests or their state, your level, the hearthstone ready or used, the flight paths you know or the bind (`Travel.lua`'s `TV.ver`), a hand-in, an XP number the game reported for a quest of that route (not one for any other quest: a quest giver's window or a party member's report), a hand-in in reach left behind (below), or a jump in place for a route from where you stand (a loading screen, landing from a flight, a hearth or a portal: 300 yards within a second, or another continent; not the release to a graveyard or the run back as a ghost: alive again more than 300 yards from where you died is one; and the client's first word this session on your flight paths while a continent is not read yet, as the plan before it counted every flight master). Not the XP within a level, a bag event, a zone's border, the flight master nearest you, or a trait event that changed no rank.
- A route from where you stand hands in a stop within 60 yards of you with a quest ready to hand in to that NPC first (none in the air or as a ghost; an unfinished quest's NPC in reach changes nothing), and a stop worth little (under the planner's line, 400 XP scaled) that it passes (under a minute's detour between the stops either side) on the way; only one that costs a trip goes last.
- A new plan from where you stand keeps the last one's first stop unless it is clearly better: 5% (QuestTrail's margin) in the planner's own terms, the levels gained first, then the minutes (and half a minute at least). A stop handed in, abandoned or no longer placed gives way at once, and a plan after a jump in place starts afresh from where you landed (5.4.4 as first built kept the stop from before a flight, and flew you back to it), as does one after a plan made with no place to start from (a dungeon). A stop in reach goes first and the stop kept comes next (`route.lead`), and once that hand-in is done the stop kept is first again; a stop put first only for being in reach, ahead of the stop kept (`route.firstNear`; the session's first plan and the first after a jump in place too, logged in or landed beside a hand-in: 5.4.4 as first built marked only a plan with a stop kept), is not kept for that: walked away from (all its NPCs past 90 yards for three seconds, on foot and alive; a step out and back is no leaving) the route is planned again, and the stop kept before it leads unless going back is clearly quicker (a hand-in 100 yards behind you against a trip back later: it is); kept first by that it is marked (`route.firstLeft`) and planned again each 90 yards further, until going back is no longer clearly quicker and it gives way, so a stop stays behind you as stop 1 only while going back is clearly quicker, planned again each 90 yards until it is not (5.4.4 as first built made the one plan for walking away, and the stop that won it stayed first behind you until something else changed; forcing the lead instead costs minutes: 100 yards out of Southshore with its hand-in ready, the lead first is 10 minutes against 7, R2); after a hand-in there that leaves another of its quests to do, the stop kept leads unless going back is clearly better (5.4.4 as first built kept it first, behind you, for having been in reach once). A banked route (you log out where it starts) is planned free.
- An objective's spot is kept until that objective is done or another spot of its quest is a quarter and 30 yards nearer, so walking between two never flips it; and the quest is kept, so running past another quest's spot never changes the objective.
- After a loading screen (the tram, a boat or a zeppelin, a flight's landing, the hearthstone, an instance's door, a jump in place), for 20 seconds a new way there travels on at the 5% line (below) as a way that travels already does: off the tram in Ironforge with a hand-in at Menethil Harbor, the flight you know at once, where 5.4.3 walked you the 1.2k yards through the mountains that a straight line is. Walking is still a straight line: with no flight known, walking straight is the plan.
- The arrow's way there (`Travel.lua`) is planned again on a new zone as on walking on: kept unless a new way is clearly quicker. A way that flies keeps flying while that saves 5% over walking (it takes 15% and a minute to start), so standing near the cut-off never flips walk and flight. The golden path has a band at each edge: your trails are found within 80 yards of you and kept to 110 while a way is shown; you join the way within 20 yards and leave it past 35. The way is anchored at its point nearest you on the trail itself (5.4.3 anchored it where you stood, and a corner cut was pointed back at until the next search), and what is drawn joins it further on: at the furthest point within 80 yards of you, as far as the way stays within reach (`GP.Aim`, worked out at every look without a search); straight to the place when it is within reach or nearer than that point, or, off the way, when going straight costs less than rejoining. The strokes are 6 pixels (8 before; the Map page's 4, 5 before), a gap short of each pin they join, softened where they cross another quest's icon and only there (a line for each part: 5.4.4 as first built dimmed the whole stroke), and under every quest icon (the Map page's canvas orders its pins' levels as the world map does). The minimap draws the 160 trail segments nearest you and the 120 objective spots nearest you.
- Zephras Isle, and any open map off the two continents, is a land of its own: the client places it by its own world's id (`Model.lua`, `M.World`: a zone map whose world is no instance; a dungeon's map still places nothing), Data.lua's NPCs there carry the same, and with no flight master there the route, the arrow and the minutes walk there. Its quests are in Now, Available and Highest XP on a card of their own. A leg to or from it with no known way (no boat or portal QuestBank knows) counts no minutes and reads "No known way there"; the route crosses as few times as it can, and what comes after is past the first hour.
- `/qb trace on` (not listed in game; off by default) notes each re-plan and what changed, each jump in place, the target before and after with where you stood (and a jump that picks the quest afresh), each change of the minimap's target with its distance, each re-plan of the arrow's way (forced or not, and why) and the golden path's searches a minute, in `QuestBankDB.trace` (the last 200 lines, in the saved file). `/qb trace print` shows the last 30; `/qb trace off` stops and clears it.

The data, all raw inputs in `research/` (gitignored):

- `travel.py` builds each faction's travel network from the Forever client's taxi tables (`research/wago/<build>/`, fetched by `tools/fetch_wago.py`) and knows where Forever redrew a map (`research/wago/era/UiMapAssignment.csv`).
- `cmangos.py` extracts chains, quest givers, spawn points and item starts from `research/cmangos/ClassicDB.sql.gz` (github.com/cmangos/classic-db, Full_DB) into `research/questbank/cmangos.json`, and the sellers of Simple Wood and Flint and Tinder (`npc_vendor`, `npc_vendor_template`) with the flight masters' faction templates (`--sellers` writes only those into it).
- `gen_data.py` writes `QuestBank/Data.lua` from those (with `D.CAMP_SELL`, the camp bar's Classic general goods vendors, each placed as a Classic spawn is and given to the side whose flight masters wear its faction template, else the side of the flight point nearest it; and `D.SLEEP_CHAIN`, the sleeping bag's quest chain), the Wowhead Forever quest data (`all3.json`, `det2.json`, `det3.json`, `wowhead.json`, `extra.json`) and NPC tooltips (`npcloc.json`; `--fetch` reads the missing ones through `npcloc.py`). Every icon and texture is checked against the client manifest. It also writes `GAPS.md`, the list of quests it can't fully plan yet. From the client's quest table (QuestV2) it takes the completion bits: quests that share one are done together, so they rule each other out (D.EXCL). Quests no list has yet are written by hand in `HAND`, and what Blizzard's notes changed that nobody has read since gets a tooltip line (D.NOTE), each with its source beside it in the code. An NPC on an open map off the two continents (a world of its own that is no instance, with a zone map over it, from the client's Map and UiMap tables: `OPEN_MAPS`, Zephras Isle's 2991) gets that world's id and its yards, as the client places it, and no flight master; the quests of a zone there get a card of their own, numbered after the others.
- `npcloc.py --quests=ID,...` reads Wowhead Forever's quest tooltips into `qtips.json` (name, text and what the quest asks for: no level, XP or NPC id). `lookfor.json` holds the ids of quests we went looking for; GAPS.md says for each one still missing why. Wowhead's quest pages (`mapper.py`) have turned scripts away since 2026-10-04 (CloudFront "Request blocked", again on 2026-10-08), while nether.wowhead.com's tooltips answer. Every Wowhead request, from both scripts, keeps 3.2 s from the last.
- `boss_spots.py` places the Dungeon Journal's bosses on the floor art and gives them a creature model, from the Classic database's spawns and templates (`boss_spots.json`). A boss's floor is the room it stands in: `wmo_floors.py` reads MoP Classic's dungeon buildings (the WMO groups each floor of the game's own map is drawn for) from wago.tools into `wmo_floors.json`, and boss_spots.py looks each spawn up in them; the rectangle of that floor then gives the spot on the art. The upper spire's old map (Blackrock Spire's seventh art, which no client table places anything on) has a rectangle fitted by hand (EXTRA); a boss an event summons stands in for the brazier, trigger or room it appears at (STAND_IN). `forever_npcs.py` reads the NPC and display ids of the bosses in Forever's own dungeons from their Wowhead Forever pages (`forever_npcs.json`; on 2026-10-14 the pages answered a script that says who it is, 3.2 s apart; a refusal stops it). gen_data.py adds the game's encounter order, the old journal's art by name and the dungeons' covers, and fails when more bosses than `FACELESS_BOSS_MAX` have no face.

XP model: Forever quest XP = the base from the game's QuestXP table (identical to Classic) x the quest's multiplier (from each Wowhead Forever quest page), then the Classic grey rule (full to 5 levels above, 80/60/40/20%, 10% from 10 above), rounded the way the server does (to 5 up to 100, to 10 up to 500, to 25 up to 1,000, to 50 above). When the game reports a quest's XP (quest log below the cap, the quest window, a hand-in), that number wins. Since 2026-10-01 (the level-30 build) dungeon quests pay "50% less extra experience beyond normal quest values"; gen_data.py applies 1 + (mult - 1) x NERF (0.5) to every multiplier above 1 read before that date and sets flag 256, until Wowhead Forever's pages are re-read (then NERF = 1, READ = that date). What the game paid after the cut (players' hand-ins and own quest-window readings, merged by probe_pull.py into disc.json's "seen" votes, build >= NERF_BUILD) confirms or overrides each number: flag 512, and GAPS.md lists the corrections. The 2026-10-08 build raised the XP of dungeon kills by about 20% and works out party XP from each player's own level again (Blizzard's notes); neither touches quest XP, and QuestBank models quest XP only. That build did change Elixir of Pain's XP and raise the level of the quests around Bandarion Keep; until players' quest logs show the new numbers, D.NOTE says so in their tooltips.
